The headline number

A first-time visitor who does the Empire State Building, the Statue of Liberty ferry, the 9/11 Museum and one Broadway show pays roughly $200–$240 per person in tickets alone. Five observation decks cost $220–$260 if you climb more than one — the single easiest place to overspend in New York.

Why this page exists

Prices are the hardest part of planning NYC

Every attraction quotes a "from" price, then adds dates, sunset slots, exhibitions and fees. Most guides copy last year's numbers. This index records what the official sellers were quoting on one date, for every headline sight, in one place.

Use it to sanity-check a pass, decide whether a second observation deck is worth it, or budget a trip without opening twelve tabs. Because it is a dataset rather than an article, other sites and writers can cite it — please link back if you do.

Methodology

How the index is built

A short method note, because a dataset without one is just a list.

Sources. Each price was read from the official operator's own booking page on 2 November 2026 — not from a reseller and not from a previous edition of this page. Where the official page was inaccessible to automated checks (the Met, SUMMIT), the figure was cross-checked against the operator's partner listing and is labelled accordingly on the individual guide.

What "from" means. The lowest adult price available on the date checked, before taxes and booking fees. Dynamic pricing means the same ticket can cost materially more at weekends, school holidays and sunset slots. Ranges in the table reflect tickets we saw on sale that week.

What is excluded. Food, transport, hotels, add-ons (102nd floor, Crown, City Climb, The Beam) and packages. Those are covered in the individual attraction guides and in the passes comparison.

Updates. The index is re-checked quarterly and whenever an operator announces a price change; the checked date at the top always reflects the latest sweep.

Questions

Price index questions

Why are the prices different from what I see at checkout?

Dynamic pricing. The index records the lowest adult “from” price seen on the operator's booking page on the checked date. Weekend, holiday and sunset slots cost more, and booking fees can be added at checkout.

Are these prices per person?

Yes — adult, per person, before tax and fees. Child and senior prices are listed in the notes and on each attraction's own guide where the operator publishes them.

Which attraction is the best value?

By sheer hours of entertainment per dollar, the free ones win: Central Park, the High Line, the Brooklyn Bridge and the Staten Island Ferry. Among paid sights, the Met and AMNH give the most time for the money.

Can I reuse this table on my site?

Yes, under CC BY 4.0 — credit NYCTrove, link to this page and keep the checked date. The CSV download is there to make that easy.

How often is the index updated?

Quarterly, plus whenever an operator announces a price change. The checked date at the top of the page always reflects the latest sweep.
