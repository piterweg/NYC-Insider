The Staten Island Ferry, explained

It is free, it runs around the clock and it passes the Statue of Liberty. It is also a commuter boat, not a sightseeing cruise — which is exactly why it is worth knowing how to use it.

How it works

Walk on, walk off, come back

Board at the Whitehall Terminal in Lower Manhattan and cross to St. George on Staten Island. The ride takes about 25 minutes each way. You do not need a ticket, a reservation or a pass; you walk on and walk off, and you can return on the next boat.

Most visitors ride it as a round trip for the harbour views, stay on the boat at St. George, and come straight back. That is allowed and costs nothing.

◆

The view

The ferry passes the Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island. The best views are from the open decks; for the outbound trip, the right side usually gives the Statue, but move around — both sides are worth it.

Practical

Five things to know

  • 01

    It is not the Statue of Liberty ferry

    The free ferry passes the Statue but does not stop at Liberty Island. To land there, book the official Statue City Cruises ferry separately.

  • 02

    It is a commuter boat

    Weekday rush hours are busy with residents. Go mid-morning or at the weekend for a calmer crossing.

  • 03

    Stand outside

    The indoor seats are fine, but the open decks are the point. Bring a jacket in winter.

  • 04

    Check the last boats

    It runs overnight, but frequency drops. Do not assume a boat every few minutes late at night.

  • 05

    Combine it with Lower Manhattan

    Whitehall is next to Battery Park, the 9/11 Memorial and the subway. Pair the ferry with a downtown morning.

Questions

Staten Island Ferry questions

Is the Staten Island Ferry really free?

Yes. There is no fare, no ticket and no booking for foot passengers.

Can I see the Statue of Liberty from the ferry?

Yes, the ferry passes it, but it does not stop at Liberty Island. For a landing, book the official Statue ferry separately.

Do I need to get off at Staten Island?

No. You can stay on for the return trip, though most people walk around the terminal for a few minutes.

Is it accessible?

Yes, both terminals and the boats are accessible for wheelchairs and strollers.
