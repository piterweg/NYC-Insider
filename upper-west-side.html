Manhattan · Upper West Side

The Upper West Side, from museum to river

The Upper West Side is where New Yorkers go to live rather than to sightsee, which is exactly why it works as a visitor: a world-class museum, the quiet west side of Central Park, the Hudson at the end of the street and a Broadway full of delis and diners.

First impressions

A residential neighbourhood with a world-class museum

The Upper West Side runs from Columbus Circle up to 110th Street, between Central Park and the Hudson. It is dense, leafy and domestic, and its pace is set by schools, dog walkers and the museum.

The spine is Broadway, which cuts diagonally across the grid and carries most of the neighbourhood's food and shops; Columbus and Amsterdam Avenues run parallel with the restaurants and cafés. The park is on one side, the river on the other, and almost everything you want is within a 20-minute walk.

It is also the most family-friendly part of Manhattan. Central Park's west side has playgrounds and ball fields, the museum is built for children and the streets are stroller-friendly by Manhattan standards. If you are choosing between the Upper East and Upper West Sides with children, this is the easier one.

What to see

The essentials

Three places explain most of why people come, and all three are within walking distance.

01

The American Museum of Natural History

The museum anchors the neighbourhood: dinosaur halls, the blue whale, the Rose Center and the Hayden Planetarium. General admission is from $28, or from $43 with one ticketed exhibition. Plan your visit at amnh.org.

02

Central Park from the west side

Enter at 72nd or 86th Street for Strawberry Fields, the Lake and the paths around Bethesda. Heckscher playground, further south, is the big one for children. The park is free; check hours and entrances.

03

Riverside Park and Columbia

The Hudson waterfront path is the neighbourhood's quiet edge, best at sunset. North along Broadway, Columbia University's campus sits in Morningside Heights, a few stops on the 1 or a long walk.

An afternoon here

A day on the Upper West Side

Museum first, park in the afternoon, river at sunset; the whole day is walkable.

  • 09:30 — AMNH at opening

    Use the 81st Street entrance, where the B/C station opens into the museum, and go straight to the dinosaurs before the halls fill.

  • 12:00 — Lunch near the museum

    Zabar's for a bagel and coffee, or a diner lunch on Broadway; both are a short walk west.

  • 13:30 — Central Park's west side

    Walk east into the park for Strawberry Fields and the Lake; Heckscher playground for children is further south.

  • 16:00 — Riverside Park

    Head west to the Hudson for the waterfront path and the sunset over New Jersey.

  • 18:30 — Dinner or a show

    Columbus Avenue for a neighbourhood dinner, or the Beacon Theatre on Broadway at 74th Street for a concert.

Practical

Getting there and getting it right

72nd Street is the main station, served by the 1, 2 and 3. The 1 also stops at 79th and 86th Streets, and the B/C at 81st–Museum of Natural History and 86th. The B/C station at 81st opens directly into the museum. From Midtown the ride is 10–15 minutes, and the neighbourhood is flat and easy to walk.

Weekday mornings are the calmest, especially at the museum; weekends and rainy days are the busiest. The park is best early and the river at sunset. The museum is open daily from 10am to 5.30pm and closes on Thanksgiving and 25 December.

◆

Use the 81st Street entrance

The B/C station opens into the museum, which saves the queue at Central Park West. Arrive at opening to see the dinosaurs before the school groups.

◷

Book museum tickets ahead

AMNH uses timed entry. General admission is from $28, or from $43 with one ticketed exhibition; residents of New York, New Jersey and Connecticut can pay what they wish with ID.

★

Save the river for sunset

Riverside Park faces west, so the end of the day is the neighbourhood's best free show. Walk down to the water from 72nd or 79th Street.

Questions

Upper West Side questions

Is the Upper West Side good for families?

Yes. It is the most family-friendly part of Manhattan: the natural history museum, Central Park's playgrounds, Riverside Park and streets that are easy with a stroller. The Upper East Side is quieter in the evening, but the west side is easier with children.

How much does the American Museum of Natural History cost?

General admission is from $28, or from $43 with one ticketed exhibition. Residents of New York, New Jersey and Connecticut can pay what they wish at the desk with ID, and the Hayden Planetarium's Space Show is a paid add-on. Prices change, so confirm before booking.

How do I get to the Upper West Side?

The 1, 2 and 3 stop at 72nd Street; the 1 also stops at 79th and 86th, and the B/C at 81st Street and 86th. The B/C station at 81st Street opens directly into the natural history museum.

Upper West Side or Upper East Side?

Choose the west side for the natural history museum, Central Park's playgrounds and the Hudson; choose the east side for the Met and Museum Mile. Both are residential and calm in the evenings.

Where should I eat on the Upper West Side?

Zabar's for bagels and smoked fish, Tom's Restaurant for diner food, Levain Bakery for the chocolate-chip cookie, and Columbus Avenue for neighbourhood restaurants. It is a local dining scene rather than a destination one.
