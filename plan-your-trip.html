Plan your trip

Build a New York trip that works

The order you book things, the area you sleep in and the pace you accept decide more about a New York trip than any list of sights. Here is how to put the pieces together before you spend anything.

Start with length

How long to stay

Three days is a long weekend: two Manhattan sightseeing days and one neighbourhood or Brooklyn morning. Five days is the length we recommend to almost everyone — enough for the icon sights, two museums, a full Brooklyn day and one proper night out.

Seven days adds Queens food, the Bronx, Staten Island and a slow morning or two. The mistake to avoid is adding days without adding area: with three days, do not try to see all five boroughs; with seven, you can.

Booking order

What to book, and when

Most of a New York trip can be decided at the last minute. Four things cannot. Work down this list in order.

Book the area, not just the hotel

A hotel is only as useful as the subway line nearest its door. Ten minutes from a station you need beats a nicer building twenty-five minutes from one. Read recent reviews for noise before comparing prices.

Where to stay

Four bases, honestly compared

There is no single best neighbourhood — only the best one for your trip. These four cover most visits.

AreaWhat it feels likeBest forTrade-offs
MidtownIcons, theatre, Grand Central and the most hotel choice in the cityFirst visits, short trips, theatre nightsCrowds and traffic by day; tourist-priced restaurants. The quietest blocks are east of Fifth Avenue.
Greenwich Village & SoHoBrownstones, jazz, cast-iron streets, independent shopsFood, nightlife and walking downtownSmaller rooms and fewer big hotels; weekend nights can be loud.
Upper West SideCentral Park, the American Museum of Natural History, Lincoln Center, neighbourhood restaurantsFamilies, calm evenings, park morningsFifteen to twenty-five minutes from the downtown sights; fewer late-night options.
BrooklynSpace, food, waterfront and a slower pace — Brooklyn Heights, DUMBO, Williamsburg and Park Slope all differSecond visits, longer stays, better valueTwenty to thirty-five minutes into Midtown. Check which line you are on before you book.

What a room costs, and what the tax adds

Quoted nightly rates move constantly with demand. As a rough guide, dorm beds run from around $60–120 a night, budget hotel rooms from around $180–280, mid-range rooms from around $300–500 and upscale rooms from around $600–1,200. All of these are before tax.

New York adds roughly 14.75% hotel tax plus $3.50 per night, so a $400 room costs closer to $462. Confirm the all-in figure before you commit.

◆

Stay near a line you will use

For a first trip that usually means the B, D, F, M, N, Q, R or W through Midtown, or the 1, 2, 3 on the west side. For Brooklyn, the A, C, F, 2, 3, 4, 5 and L cover most of the places you will want.

☂

Read the fine print

Some cheaper rooms share a bathroom or sit above a bar. Neither is a problem if you know in advance. Filter by what you actually need rather than by star rating.

◆

The three costs that decide everything

Accommodation, food and tickets. Transport is cheap by international standards — around $36 a week per person on the subway — so do not let a $15 cab ride dominate your planning.

☂

Shoulder season is the discount

Late January and early February are the cheapest weeks of the year for rooms and the quietest for museums. November, early December and the second half of April are the value sweet spots for weather versus price.

Questions

Planning questions we get a lot

How far ahead should I book a New York trip?

Book flights and the hotel as soon as your dates are fixed, and four to six months ahead for Thanksgiving, Christmas or New Year. Broadway and timed sights can wait until eight to twelve weeks out. Restaurant reservations open two to four weeks ahead for most places.

Should I stay in Manhattan or Brooklyn?

Manhattan is simpler for a first trip, especially Midtown or the Upper West Side. Brooklyn offers more space and better food for the money, at the cost of twenty to thirty-five minutes on the subway.

How much will the whole trip cost?

Use the daily bands above and add your hotel, flights and one or two big tickets. A mid-range five-day trip for two people, excluding flights, typically lands around $5,500–6,000 including a mid-range hotel and its tax.

Do I need to book restaurants?

Only the one or two dinners that would genuinely disappoint you if you missed them. Everything else is easier to decide the day before, and New York's best meals are often the ones you did not plan.

Is a sightseeing pass worth it?

It depends on how many paid attractions you will actually visit. Five or more in a short trip is where a pass starts to win; two or three is usually cheaper bought separately.

What should I do on arrival day?

Keep it light. Check in, leave the bags, walk your neighbourhood and eat somewhere close. Save the first big sight for the first full day, when a delayed flight can no longer break the plan.
