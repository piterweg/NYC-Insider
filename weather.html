What the weather does

Four seasons, four different cities

New York's weather is not extreme, but it changes fast: a warm October can flip to a cold November, and summer humidity makes the subway feel tropical.

Winter is cold and windy rather than deep-snow cold; summer is hot and humid with afternoon storms. Spring arrives slowly and autumn is the most reliable season for walking. The averages below are a planning tool, not a forecast — always check the week of your trip.

Packing

What to wear, by season

New York is a walking city; footwear matters more than a third jacket.

Spring (Mar–May)

Layers and a rain jacket

Mornings are cold, afternoons mild. A packable waterproof layer and broken-in shoes cover most days.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

Light clothes, heavy water

Humidity is the story. Linen, a refillable bottle and a plan that avoids the subway at 5pm.

Autumn (Sep–Nov)

The best walking weather

Mild days, cool evenings. A light jumper and a jacket are enough until November.

Winter (Dec–Feb)

Windproof beats thick

The wind between buildings matters more than the temperature. Hat, gloves and a windproof coat.

Rain plan

When the forecast turns

Questions

Weather questions

What is the best month to visit New York?

May, September and October are the most comfortable for walking. July and August are hot and humid; January and February are cold but cheaper.

Does it snow in New York?

Snow is possible from December to March but not guaranteed. Some winters bring several storms, others almost none. Check the week-of forecast before you pack.

How reliable is the live forecast?

The panel pulls Open-Meteo data for Central Park and caches it for 15 minutes. Forecasts beyond three days are indicative, not certain.

What should I pack in winter?

A windproof coat, hat and gloves matter more than a very thick jumper: the wind between buildings makes the cold feel sharper.

Is summer unbearable?

It can be humid, but mornings and evenings are manageable. Plan museums for the middle of the day and keep water with you.
