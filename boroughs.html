Boroughs

Five boroughs, five different cities

Manhattan is the postcard. Brooklyn is the culture. Queens is the food. The Bronx is the history. Staten Island is the breathing space. Here is how to choose where to spend your time — and why it is worth crossing the river at least once.

How to travel between them

Crossing the river is easier than you think

People treat Brooklyn and Queens like day trips. They are 10–25 minutes from Midtown on the subway, and the ferry is often faster and always more pleasant.

The subway runs 24 hours. The Staten Island Ferry is free and runs all night. The Hudson and East River ferries cost $5 but give you the skyline from the water. Taxis between boroughs are expensive because of tunnel tolls and congestion pricing — use the subway instead.

Getting around NYC
◆

The best free skyline view

The Staten Island Ferry. Twenty-five minutes each way, no ticket, and it passes the Statue of Liberty. Take it at sunset and come back on the next boat.

⌁

The best food crawl

The 7 train through Queens: lunch in Jackson Heights, then continue to Flushing for the food court at New World Mall. Bring an appetite and no plans.

☂

A wet-weather borough day

Brooklyn Museum and the Brooklyn Botanic Garden are next to each other; MoMA PS1 and the Noguchi Museum work in the rain; the Bronx Museum is free and indoors.

Questions

Borough questions

Is it safe to visit all five boroughs?

Yes — New York is statistically one of the safest large cities in the United States. Use normal city sense: keep phones out of sight on crowded trains, avoid deserted blocks late at night, and use licensed taxis. The tourist-heavy streets in Manhattan are where pickpockets actually work.

How long does it take to get from Manhattan to Brooklyn?

Ten to twenty-five minutes by subway depending on where you start and finish. The L from Union Square to Williamsburg is two stops; the 4/5 to Bowling Green plus a walk gets you to the Brooklyn Bridge.

Can I do Queens and the Bronx in one day?

You can, but you should not. Each deserves half a day minimum; the food alone in Queens is a reason to stay longer. Our 7-day itinerary gives them separate days.

Which borough has the best food?

Queens for variety and value, Brooklyn for restaurants and bakeries, Manhattan for fine dining, the Bronx for Italian-American and Arthur Avenue. The honest answer: eat in all of them — see our food guide.
