Where to stay in Brooklyn

The borough with the best food, the best waterfront and the better-value rooms — if you choose the right neighbourhood. Nine areas compared, the tax explained, and whether Brooklyn is the right base.

9 areas compared10–20 min to ManhattanPrices before taxChecked November 2026
Why Brooklyn

The other side of the river

Brooklyn gives you more room, better restaurants and a street that feels lived in, for less than a comparable Manhattan hotel. The trade is a subway ride of ten to twenty minutes, or an hour from Coney Island.

For a first trip built around the Empire State Building and Times Square, Midtown is still simpler. For families, longer stays and anyone who would rather eat well than sleep beside a landmark, Brooklyn is the better base — see the full comparison if you are deciding between boroughs.

The real cost

What the tax adds

New York adds roughly 14.75% hotel tax plus $3.50 per night on top of the quoted rate, and any facility fee is charged on top again. A $260 room in Williamsburg becomes about $302 a night; a $180 room in Bushwick is closer to $210; a $450 room in DUMBO lands near $520.

Ask for the all-in total — room, tax, occupancy fee and facility fee — and compare that figure. Our budget guide shows where accommodation sits in a daily budget.

◆

The tax is not in the rate

Most quoted rates exclude tax and fees. A hotel that looks forty dollars cheaper can be the more expensive one once 14.75% and the nightly fee are added.

Booking

How to book a Brooklyn hotel

The rate is only part of the decision. These four things change what you pay.

Direct or agent

Compare the final number

Hotel sites usually match the big booking sites and are easier to change. Compare the all-in total, then take the better cancellation terms.

Location

Pick the line, then the hotel

A hotel near the A/C or the 2/3 beats a nicer building two avenues away. Check the walk to the station and where the line goes at night.

The L train

Have a plan B for Williamsburg

Weekend work on the L is common. If you stay in Williamsburg or Bushwick, know the J/M/Z or ferry alternative.

Room type

Check what kind of property it is

Brooklyn has hotels, inns and serviced apartments, with different policies on minimum stays and deposits. Read the listing before booking.

Questions

Staying in Brooklyn — common questions

Is Brooklyn a good base for a first trip?

It can be. Downtown Brooklyn and Brooklyn Heights are ten to twenty minutes from Lower Manhattan, and cost less than a comparable Midtown hotel. A first visit focused on the Midtown icons is simpler from Manhattan.

How long does it take to get to Manhattan?

About 10–20 minutes from Downtown Brooklyn, Brooklyn Heights and DUMBO, and about 10 minutes from Williamsburg to Union Square on the L. Park Slope and Bushwick take 25–40 minutes; Coney Island is around an hour.

Which Brooklyn neighbourhood is best for families?

Park Slope and Prospect Heights, for Prospect Park, the museum and calmer streets, or Downtown Brooklyn for larger rooms and the quickest ride in. See the Brooklyn guide for what each area is like.

Do I need a car in Brooklyn?

No. Every neighbourhood here is on the subway, and the NYC Ferry adds a waterfront route to Manhattan for $5 one-way. Parking is expensive and traffic is slow.

Is it cheaper to stay in Brooklyn than Manhattan?

Usually. Downtown Brooklyn and Williamsburg are cheaper than comparable Midtown and SoHo hotels, and the outer neighbourhoods cheaper again. The saving shrinks on the waterfront.
