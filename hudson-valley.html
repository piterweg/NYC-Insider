Day trip · Hudson Valley

The Hudson Valley in a day

The easiest day out of New York that is not a city: eighty minutes up the Hudson Line to Beacon, with a contemporary art museum, a sculpture park and a good Main Street waiting at the end of the platform.

Why the Hudson Valley

The city limits, eighty minutes out

Hudson Valley is the day trip for when you want a landscape rather than another skyline. The train from Grand Central follows the river north, and Beacon station is close enough to the galleries and Main Street that you can walk off the platform and start.

Main Street Beacon, with the restaurants, coffee and shops you want for lunch, sits between the museum and the station. If you have done Dia and Storm King before, continue north to Cold Spring, a smaller village with antique shops and the Hudson Highlands trailheads. The whole day needs no car: buy a Metro-North ticket before boarding, check the last sensible train back, and the only decision left is how long to give Storm King.

◆

Check Storm King's season

Storm King Art Center is open from April to November and is a short taxi or seasonal shuttle ride from Beacon station. Check current opening dates and transport options before you plan the day around it.

◷

Grand Central, not Penn Station

Metro-North leaves from Grand Central Terminal; Amtrak leaves from Penn Station. They are not interchangeable, and the difference is a missed train.

Getting there

Getting there: the Hudson Line

Metro-North runs from Grand Central Terminal along the east bank of the Hudson. Beacon is the practical stop for Dia Beacon and Main Street; Cold Spring is one further for the village and the trails.

What to do

What the day is really about

Two art stops, one Main Street and, if you have the time, a village further up the river. Choose Dia and Storm King if it is your first visit.

Art

Dia Beacon

Large-scale contemporary art in a converted factory beside the river. The scale of the building is as much of the experience as the work.

Sculpture

Storm King Art Center

A sculpture park set into rolling hills, with works along paths and lawns rather than in rooms. Open April to November; allow at least two hours.

Lunch

Main Street, Beacon

Coffee, bakeries, restaurants and small shops between the station and the river. It is where the day naturally pauses, and the reason Beacon works without a car.

Village

Cold Spring

A smaller river town further north, with antique shops, a waterfront and hills behind it. Good for a second visit or when Dia is enough art for one day.

Walking

The Hudson Highlands

Trails above the river, reached from Cold Spring and nearby stations. Check trail conditions and daylight before setting out.

Sample day

A realistic Hudson Valley day

One full day from Grand Central, paced around two art stops and a long lunch. Times are indicative — check current train times before you go.

  • 08:30 — Train from Grand Central

    Take a Hudson Line train north. Sit on the left-hand side for the river views once you leave the city.

  • 10:00 — Dia Beacon

    Walk from the station to the museum and give it two hours. Start upstairs and work down as the morning crowds spread out.

  • 12:30 — Lunch on Main Street

    Beacon's main street is a short walk from the station. Eat properly here — the next stop is a sculpture park with limited food.

  • 14:00 — Storm King Art Center

    Take a taxi or the seasonal shuttle from Beacon. Two hours across the grounds is a good first visit.

  • 17:00 — Back to Beacon

    Return to the station, get a coffee on Main Street and take an evening train back. Alternatively, continue north to Cold Spring if you have an early start and want the village.

◆

Go on a weekday if you can

The trains and galleries are calmer, and Storm King is a different place with a few hundred people rather than a few thousand.

◷

Check Storm King twice

Once for the season, once for the transport. It closes from November to March and the shuttle does not run all year.

☂

Wear something you can walk in

Storm King is a park, not a gallery, and the ground is uneven. Proper shoes change the experience here.

Questions

Day-trip questions

Can you visit the Hudson Valley as a day trip from New York?

Yes, easily. Metro-North from Grand Central reaches Beacon in about an hour and twenty minutes, and Dia Beacon, Main Street and Storm King all fit into one day. Take an early train.

Do I need a car for a Hudson Valley day trip?

Not for Beacon or Cold Spring. Storm King is a few miles from Beacon station and is reached by taxi or a seasonal shuttle; check current options before relying on it.

What is the best day for a Hudson Valley day trip?

A weekday or Saturday. Weekdays are quietest; Saturdays have more going on in the villages. Check current fares and schedules.

Is the Hudson Valley worth visiting in winter?

Partly. Storm King closes from November to March, so a winter day is built around Dia Beacon and the villages. Check current opening days and train times.
