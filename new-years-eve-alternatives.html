New Year's Eve Alternatives

Times Square is the famous way to see midnight in New York, not the only one. A booked table, a show, a comedy club or a Brooklyn waterfront give you the same countdown without the six hours of standing — and in most cases you need to decide weeks, not hours, in advance.

The short version

Choose the midnight you actually want

The alternatives fall into three groups: eat and drink somewhere booked, watch a show, or find a free view and count down outside. Each costs a different amount of money, comfort and advance planning, and none of them involves standing in a viewing pen for six hours.

Restaurants are the most popular alternative. Most places that open on 31 December serve a fixed menu rather than the normal à la carte list, often with two sittings and a prepaid deposit; the details vary by venue, so read the terms when you book. Broadway theatres vary too — some shows perform on 31 December, some do not, and schedules are published by the productions rather than the city. TKTS sells same-day seats at up to 50% off; hours are published at tdf.org. Comedy clubs sell tickets in advance for special New Year's Eve line-ups; check each venue.

For a free alternative, the Brooklyn waterfront is the obvious choice. Brooklyn Bridge Park and the DUMBO shoreline look straight back at Lower Manhattan, and you do not need a ticket to stand there — though you should expect crowds, cold and limited toilets. Midnight runs and fireworks are organised separately each year and are often announced only in December; check the official organiser's site before planning around one. Whatever you choose, plan the journey home before midnight: the subway runs around the clock, but stations near big events are busy and some entrances close.

What's on

Three ways to avoid the pens

Booked, seated or free — pick one and commit early. The one thing all three share is that leaving the planning to 31 December rarely works well.

01

A restaurant table

Fixed menus, two sittings and prepaid deposits are normal on 31 December. Book weeks ahead, read the cancellation terms and confirm what the price includes before you pay.

02

A show or a comedy club

Some Broadway shows perform on New Year's Eve, and comedy clubs run special line-ups. Schedules and tickets come from the venue; TKTS can help with same-day seats at up to 50% off.

03

A Brooklyn viewpoint

Brooklyn Bridge Park and the DUMBO waterfront are free, open and give you the skyline at midnight. Expect cold, crowds and no official event unless one is announced — and check before you rely on fireworks.

Plan it

Planning the night

Decide what you are willing to spend and how long you are willing to stand, then book what matches.

  1. Choose your format: dinner, show, comedy, free viewpoint or a house party. Each has a different booking deadline, and the seated options sell out first.
  2. Book the table or tickets as early as you can. Fixed-menu dinners and New Year's Eve performances are usually prepaid and non-refundable.
  3. Read the small print before paying: sitting times, what the price includes, cancellation terms and whether the venue expects you to stay until midnight.
  4. Check show times before booking. Some performances finish early enough to reach a viewpoint by midnight; others run through the countdown.
  5. Plan your route home. The subway runs 24 hours, but expect crowds and street closures around Times Square and the waterfront, and allow extra time.
◆

Book in November, not December

New Year's Eve tables, shows and comedy tickets are sold months ahead. Waiting until the week before leaves you with the expensive seats or the walk-in queue.

☂

Dress for a long night

Expect temperatures around 45°F (7°C) by day and 34°F (1°C) at night, with 11.4 wet days in December. A viewpoint or a queue outdoors needs a proper coat, hat and gloves.

◷

Check before relying on fireworks

Midnight fireworks and organised runs are announced separately each year. Check the official organiser's site in December rather than assuming last year's event is happening.

Local rhythm

How locals spend 31 December

  • 01

    Book early, eat late

    New Yorkers who go out tend to book a table for the second sitting and stay put. It is warmer, cheaper than a bar crawl and you keep your seat at midnight.

  • 02

    The waterfront is the free seat

    Brooklyn Bridge Park and the DUMBO shoreline are the local answer to a free midnight view: skyline, river and no ticket. Go early, bring something warm to sit on and expect a crowd by 11pm.

  • 03

    Or stay in

    Plenty of people who live here watch the ball drop on television and go out on 1 January instead. New Year's Day is quiet in the morning, the parks are empty and restaurants are easier to book.

Questions

New Year's Eve alternative questions

Do I need tickets for the Times Square ball drop?

No. The ball drop is free and unticketed, with first-come entry to the viewing areas. Arrive in the early afternoon, expect security screening and plan to stand for hours in the cold.

What are the best alternatives to Times Square on New Year's Eve?

A booked restaurant, a Broadway or comedy show, a Brooklyn viewpoint or a private party. Seated options need booking weeks ahead; free viewpoints need warm clothes and an early arrival.

Is Broadway open on New Year's Eve?

Some productions perform on 31 December and some do not. Schedules are published by the individual shows. TKTS sells discounted same-day seats; hours are on TDF's site.

Where can I see fireworks in New York on New Year's Eve?

Fireworks and midnight events change from year to year. Check the official organiser before planning around one. The Brooklyn waterfront is the reliable free skyline view, with or without fireworks.

How do I get home after midnight?

The subway runs 24 hours, but stations near Times Square and the waterfront are busy and some entrances close. Decide your route beforehand, allow extra time and expect to walk a few blocks to a train.
