Observation decks

The best observation deck in New York

Five decks, five different versions of the same city, and headline prices within about $10 of each other. Here is which one to choose for the trip you are actually taking.

5 decks comparedFrom $42 to $51Open dailyPrices checked November 2026

The short answer

For most first trips the best single deck is Top of the Rock (from $42): it puts the Empire State in the frame and gives you Central Park on axis. Choose the Empire State Building (from $46) for the open-air terrace, One World Observatory ($51) for the harbour, Edge (around $44–69) for the glass floor, or SUMMIT (around $44–62) for the most photogenic interior.

The five decks

Five decks, five different cities

All five are good. What changes is the angle, the building you are standing on and what ends up in your photographs.

Top of the Rock and the Empire State face each other across Midtown. From Rockefeller Center you see the Empire State; from the Empire State you see everything except itself, from an open-air terrace. Edge sits west at Hudson Yards with a glass floor, SUMMIT turns the trip into a mirrored art installation above Grand Central, and One World Observatory looks south over the harbour and the Statue of Liberty.

Height is not the deciding factor. The tallest deck is not automatically the best view, because the tallest building is usually the one you cannot see. Decide what you want in the frame, then book the deck that provides it — and book one, not three.

How to choose

Choose by the trip, not the deck

Each of these is the right answer for a different itinerary.

Best first deck

One ticket, first trip

Take Top of the Rock from $42. You get the skyline photograph everyone recognises, Central Park running north and both rivers at the edges. Book a late-afternoon slot into dusk, and accept that sunset is the busiest hour.

Lower Manhattan

Harbour, bridges and the Statue

One World Observatory, from $51, is the deck for the harbour and the Statue of Liberty. Pair it with the 9/11 Memorial & Museum, a short walk away. Go in the morning for the clearest light.

Photographers

Glass floors and mirror rooms

Edge and SUMMIT are the two most photogenic decks. Choose Edge for the outdoor cantilever and glass floor over Hudson Yards, SUMMIT for the mirrored installations above Grand Central. Both are date-priced, so check your day.

Costs and booking

What the five decks cost and how to book

Prices are dynamic. Adult admission runs from $42 at Top of the Rock to $51 at One World Observatory, with Edge around $44–69 and SUMMIT around $44–62. Sunset slots and holiday dates sit at the top of those ranges. The 102nd floor, The Beam and SKYLIFT, and SUMMIT's glass elevator are paid extras.

Book direct with each operator, not through resellers. Every deck uses timed entry and sunset slots go first. A CityPASS covers the Empire State's 86th floor, Top of the Rock and Edge among its ten options — if you already plan two decks, the pass can be cheaper; test that on our pass comparison.

Book one deck, not three

The most common first-trip mistake is paying for three skyline views. Pick the frame you want and spend the difference on a better meal or a second show.

☂

Weather matters more than the deck

Haze flattens the view from any height. If the forecast is murky, move the deck to your clearest day rather than paying for a grey rectangle.

◷

Opening and closing are the calm hours

The first slot and the last hour are the quietest at every deck. Sunset is the crush — book it days ahead if that is the light you want.

Questions

Observation deck questions

Which is the best observation deck in New York?

For most first trips, Top of the Rock: from $42 it puts the Empire State in the frame and shows Central Park on axis. Choose the Empire State (from $46) for open air, One World Observatory ($51) for the harbour, or Edge and SUMMIT for glass floors and interiors.

Which deck has the best view of the Empire State Building?

Top of the Rock. The south side of the 70th-floor deck looks directly at the Empire State Building, close enough to fill a phone frame. No other observation deck includes it in the skyline.

Which observation deck is cheapest?

Top of the Rock is cheapest at from $42, with the Empire State's 86th floor from $46. Edge and SUMMIT overlap that range and One World Observatory is from $51. Prices are dynamic, so compare your exact date.

Which deck is least crowded?

The first slot after opening or the last hour before closing; sunset is the busiest time at every deck. The Empire State is calmest in the first 30 minutes after opening or after 20:00.

Do I need to book observation decks in advance?

Yes. All five use timed entry and sunset slots sell out first. Book direct and arrive at the start of your window.
