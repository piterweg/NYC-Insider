Borough 01 · Manhattan

Manhattan

The island everyone pictures when they picture New York — and, with a little planning, a place where you can still find quiet streets, old bars and a skyline all to yourself.

1.6 million residents23 square milesAll subway lines2–4 days
The island

The postcard, and everything underneath it

Manhattan is 13 miles long and 2 miles wide, and almost everything visitors want to see sits in the bottom half of it. The trick is to treat the island as a set of neighbourhoods rather than one long sightseeing list.

Spend your mornings on the big things — observation decks, the Statue of Liberty ferry, The Met — and your afternoons walking the streets that make the city liveable: the West Village, the Upper West Side, the Lower East Side. That is where the postcard turns into a place.

Everything below 59th Street is dense, walkable and served by dozens of subway lines. Above it, the grid is easy and the pace is calmer. You do not need a car for any of it.

Neighbourhoods

The Manhattan neighbourhoods worth your time

Nine areas that together explain most of the island. You will not do them all in one trip, and you should not try.

Midtown

Midtown

Times Square, Broadway, Grand Central and Rockefeller Center in one crowded, convenient grid. Best for a first hotel and theatre nights.

Lower Manhattan

Lower Manhattan

Wall Street, the 9/11 Memorial, Battery Park and the ferry terminals. Compact, historic, and quiet at weekends.

SoHo & Nolita

SoHo

Cast-iron facades, cobblestones and global retail. Walk Greene, Prince and Broome streets, then eat on the edge of Nolita.

SoHo guide
Greenwich Village

Greenwich Village

Brownstones, Washington Square Park, jazz basements and the crooked streets of the West Village. The best place to be at 6pm.

West Village guide
Upper East Side

Upper East Side

Museum Mile — the Met, the Guggenheim, the Neue Galerie — plus Central Park's quiet eastern edge.

Upper West Side

Upper West Side

AMNH, Lincoln Center, Zabar's and Riverside Park. Residential, family-friendly and calmer than Midtown.

Harlem

Harlem

The Apollo, the Schomburg Center, 125th Street and a deep jazz and soul-food history. Worth a guided half-day.

Harlem guide
Lower East Side

Lower East Side

Tenement history, Katz's and Russ & Daughters, and a nightlife scene that goes late. The city's immigrant story in a few blocks.

Central Park

Central Park

843 acres of free, from Bethesda Terrace to the Ramble. Enter at 72nd Street and walk north.

What to do

The essentials

Book the first three before you travel; the rest can happen on the day.

01

Central Park

Free, 843 acres, and genuinely the reason many New Yorkers stay. Start at Bethesda Terrace; end at the Ramble.

02

The Met

One of the great museums on earth. Three hours on the highlights is the right call; it is closed Wednesdays.

03

A skyline deck

Top of the Rock for the Empire State Building view; the Empire State Building for the view that includes it. Book the sunset slot early.

04

Statue of Liberty ferry

From Battery Park, with Ellis Island included. The only authorised operator is Statue City Cruises — ignore anyone selling on the street.

05

Broadway

Book ahead for a specific show, or use TKTS on the day for up to half price. The Theatre District starts at 41st Street.

06

Lower Manhattan

9/11 Memorial, Wall Street, Stone Street and the ferries. The plaza is free; the museum takes two hours.

Where to eat

Manhattan in six plates

The island is expensive, but its best-loved food is still cheap if you know where to queue.

  • ◆

    Katz's Delicatessen

    Lower East Side. Pastrami on rye, hand-carved, about $32–36. Keep your ticket.

  • ◆

    Russ & Daughters

    Lower East Side. Bagel with lox at the counter that has been doing this since 1914.

  • ◆

    Joe's Pizza

    Greenwich Village. The rare New York slice worth the reputation — about $4.50.

  • ◆

    Los Tacos No. 1

    Chelsea Market and Times Square. Adobada tacos, no seats, worth standing for.

  • ◆

    The Halal Guys

    53rd and Sixth. The chicken-and-rice platter that starts a queue every evening.

  • ◆

    Chelsea Market

    Not one restaurant but twenty, under one roof. The reliable rainy-day lunch.

One day here

One day in Manhattan

A realistic loop that hits the park, a museum and the skyline without crossing the island twice.

  • 8:30 · Central Park

    Enter at 72nd Street, walk the Mall to Bethesda Terrace and the Bow Bridge.

  • 10:30 · The Met

    Three hours on the highlights — or swap for AMNH if you are travelling with children.

  • 14:00 · Lunch on the Upper East Side

    Then take the 6 downtown toward the Village.

  • 16:00 · Greenwich Village

    Washington Square Park, Bleecker Street, a coffee, a slice.

  • 18:00 · Top of the Rock

    Book the slot before sunset for the best light on the Empire State Building.

  • 20:00 · Dinner in Midtown or the Theatre District

    Or a Broadway show if you booked ahead.

Getting there

Getting around

Every subway line passes through Manhattan. Above 59th Street, walking the grid is often faster than going underground; below it, take the train. Crosstown buses are slow — the exception is the M42 and the M57 if you are already at street level.

◆

Eat away from the squares

Restaurants within two blocks of Times Square are priced for people who will never return. Walk ten minutes west or north and the food improves immediately.

☂

Check museum closures

The Met is closed Wednesdays; MoMA's free Friday is for New York State residents; AMNH charges extra for its ticketed exhibitions.

◷

Book decks for sunset

Observation-deck sunset slots sell out days ahead. If you miss one, go at opening instead — the queues are shorter and the light is clean.

Questions

Manhattan questions

Do I need to stay in Manhattan?

Not necessarily. Brooklyn and Queens are cheaper and often nicer, and anywhere near a subway gets you into Midtown in 15–30 minutes. See where to stay.

Is Times Square worth visiting?

For fifteen minutes at dusk, yes — it is genuinely spectacular as a piece of urban theatre. For dinner, no.

How many days should I give Manhattan?

Two full days covers the icons; three or four lets you add museums and neighbourhoods. Our 5-day itinerary gives it three and a half.

What is the best free thing in Manhattan?

Central Park, followed closely by Grand Central's concourse and the Staten Island Ferry from Whitehall. All three cost nothing.
