Getting around

How New York actually moves

New York is a walking city with a very good train set underneath it. Once you understand the airport transfer, the fare cap and when a taxi is worth it, the city becomes much smaller.

From the airport

JFK, LGA and EWR to Manhattan

None of the three airports has a one-seat train to Midtown, but all are straightforward. Costs are one-way per person.

Allow more time than you think

The transfer is rarely the problem; traffic on the last two miles is. For a flight, leave three hours for an international departure and two for domestic, plus the transfer time.

Subway & OMNY

The subway, and how to pay for it

The subway runs 24 hours a day and reaches everywhere a visitor is likely to go. It is also the cheapest part of a New York trip by a wide margin.

The base fare is $3.00 for the subway and local buses (from 4 January 2026), with a reduced fare of $1.50 for eligible riders; express buses cost $7.25. Pay by tapping a contactless card, phone or watch on the turnstile reader, or use an OMNY card ($1 introductory fee) from a vending machine.

Fare capping is the part most visitors miss. Tap the same card every time and after 12 fares in any rolling seven days, further rides are free — about $36 a week, or $67 including express buses; the reduced-fare cap is $17.50. MetroCard sales and refills ended on 1 January 2026, and existing cards are accepted into 2026 only until the MTA announces a cut-off. Do not plan around buying one.

Tapping in, step by step

  • 01

    Use one card per rider

    Each person taps their own card or device. One card cannot tap two people through, and sharing breaks the fare cap.

  • 02

    Tap, wait for the green light

    Hold the card flat on the reader on top of the turnstile until it beeps and shows green, then push through.

  • 03

    Tap on buses at the front

    Board at the front and tap the reader beside the driver.

  • 04

    Keep the same card all week

    The cap follows the card, not the person or the trip.

◆

Which card is cheapest?

A contactless bank card usually avoids foreign transaction fees and hits the weekly cap automatically. Some prepaid travel cards charge per tap, so check first.

Taxis & rideshare

When a cab is worth it

Yellow cabs are metered, plentiful in Midtown and easy to flag. From JFK the fare is a flat $70 plus tolls and tip — around $90–110 in total. From LaGuardia a metered cab is around $35–55 plus extras; tips run 15–20%.

Congestion pricing adds $0.75 to each yellow-cab trip and $1.50 to each rideshare trip that enters Manhattan south of 60th Street. Rideshare prices surge; a yellow cab does not, so when the app quotes double, walk to the nearest rank. Late at night a cab is often the sensible choice for a group.

Use the Curb app to order a yellow cab, or Uber and Lyft for rideshare. At airports, follow signs to the designated pickup area rather than accepting a ride from someone who approaches you inside the terminal.

Across the water

PATH, ferries and Citi Bike

Three alternatives that are sometimes faster than the subway and always more interesting.

New Jersey

PATH

A separate train system linking Lower Manhattan and Midtown with Hoboken, Jersey City and Newark. The fare is $3.25 from 4 May 2026, or $3.10 a ride in a 10-trip bundle. It does not accept OMNY.

Check the PATH app for service changes
Ferries

Staten Island and NYC Ferry

The Staten Island Ferry is free, runs 24 hours and takes about 25 minutes each way, roughly every 30 minutes — and gives you the best free view of the Statue of Liberty. NYC Ferry costs $5.00 one way (from 19 October 2026) and runs along the East River to Brooklyn, Queens and the Bronx.

Bikes

Citi Bike

A docked bike share across Manhattan, Brooklyn and Queens. A single ride is $4.99 per 30 minutes, a day pass $25, an annual membership $239. Use the app to find a dock, and allow extra time at waterfront stations in the evening.

Helmets are not provided
Accessibility

Step-free travel takes planning

Only about 150 of the subway's 472 stations are step-free. Buses are the reliable alternative — every one is wheelchair-accessible.

Step-free stations include Times Square–42 Street, Grand Central, Fulton Street, Atlantic Avenue–Barclays Center, Flushing–Main Street, 161 Street–Yankee Stadium and Coney Island–Stillwell Avenue. For anything else, plan the route in the MTA app, which shows step-free paths and live lift status, and be ready to switch to a bus if a lift is out.

Allow extra time and check the lift status on the morning you travel. Access-A-Ride paratransit serves eligible riders but must be arranged in advance.

◆

Ask for help

Station agents can open the wide gate and direct you to the lift; bus drivers lower the ramp. New Yorkers are used to giving directions.

Apps and street sense

What to install before you land

A handful of apps covers almost everything, and much of the city has free WiFi.

  • 01

    MTA and Citymapper

    Live subway status, service changes and step-free routes; Citymapper is better for walking and transit directions.

  • 02

    Transit and NYC Ferry

    Real-time arrivals for subway, bus and ferry. The NYC Ferry app includes timetables and tickets.

  • 03

    TKTS, TodayTix, Resy and OpenTable

    Same-day theatre seats, restaurant reservations and last-minute tables.

  • 04

    Curb, Uber and Lyft

    Order a yellow cab or a rideshare.

LinkNYC kiosks provide free WiFi on many streets, and there is free WiFi on subway platforms and in public libraries.

☂

Safety at night

New York is statistically one of the safest large cities in the United States. Keep your phone out of sight on crowded platforms, watch for pickpockets in Times Square and on packed trains, and ignore anyone selling Statue of Liberty tickets on the street.

◆

Late-night rules of thumb

Stick to busy, lit streets, wait near other people, and use a cab if you are unsure of the route. 911 is for emergencies; 311 for everything else.

Questions

Getting-around questions

Can I use a contactless bank card on the subway?

Yes. Tap a contactless credit or debit card, phone or watch at any turnstile. Use the same one all week to reach the fare cap. Check whether your bank charges foreign transaction fees, as a few percent per tap adds up.

Do I still need a MetroCard?

No. Sales and refills ended on 1 January 2026; existing cards are accepted into 2026 only until the MTA announces a cut-off. Use contactless payment or an OMNY card.

What is the cheapest way from JFK to Manhattan?

AirTrain plus the E subway: $11.75 in total and 50–60 minutes to Midtown. For Penn Station, AirTrain plus the LIRR is faster at $14 off-peak and $16 peak.

How do I get to Staten Island?

The Staten Island Ferry from Whitehall Terminal in Lower Manhattan is free, runs 24 hours and takes about 25 minutes each way, roughly every 30 minutes.
