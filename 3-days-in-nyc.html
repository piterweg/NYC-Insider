Itinerary · 3 days

Three days in New York

The short-break plan: one skyline morning, one harbour day, one park-and-neighbourhood day. Every stop is clustered so you spend your time in New York, not on the F train.

3 days4–6 stops a dayPublic transportBest for first-timers
At a glance

How this plan is built

Three days is not enough to “do” New York, and any plan that pretends otherwise is a list of queues. This one gives you the icons, one proper neighbourhood and one skyline night — in an order that avoids backtracking.

  • 1

    Day 1 — Midtown, from above and below

    Land, drop bags, take the skyline in early, then let the evening pull you through Times Square to a show.

  • 2

    Day 2 — The harbour and Brooklyn

    Statue of Liberty ferry first thing, Lower Manhattan after, then walk the bridge at golden hour.

  • 3

    Day 3 — Central Park and a neighbourhood

    Park and museum in the morning; the Village, SoHo or the High Line in the afternoon depending on your taste.

The plan

Day by day

Timings assume you are staying in Midtown or Lower Manhattan and using the subway. Shift the first stop later if you are arriving from a red-eye.

01 Midtown

Arrive, go up, come down to Earth

Start with the view you came for, then walk the city at street level. Keep the afternoon loose — arrival days are unreliable.

  • Drop bags — hotel or a luggage storage near your first stop
  • Empire State Building — 86th floor observatory — go early if you can; sunset slots sell out
  • Lunch — a proper diner or deli in the Garment District; skip the tourist-menu traps on 7th Avenue
  • Bryant Park & the New York Public Library — 20 quiet minutes, free, and the best public bathroom in Midtown
  • Times Square — arrive around dusk — it is better than people admit for 15 minutes
  • Broadway show — book ahead; see our tips below for rush and TKTS options
02 Harbour & Brooklyn

Statue, memorials, and the bridge at sunset

The busiest day, but geographically tight. Buy the ferry ticket in advance and be at Battery Park before 9am.

  • Statue City Cruises ferry — Battery Park to Liberty Island and Ellis Island — 3–4 hours total
  • Lunch — Fulton Street or the Tin Building at the Seaport
  • 9/11 Memorial — the plaza is free; the museum needs 1.5–2 hours and a ticket
  • Walk the Brooklyn Bridge — from the Manhattan side, mid-to-late afternoon
  • DUMBO — Washington Street photo, Brooklyn Bridge Park, one slice at Juliana's or a coffee
  • Dinner — back in Manhattan at the Seaport, or stay in Brooklyn
03 Park & downtown

Central Park in the morning, a neighbourhood after

One museum, one long park walk, then whichever downtown neighbourhood suits you. You will finish near good food either way.

  • Central Park — enter at 72nd Street, walk the Mall to Bethesda Terrace and the Bow Bridge
  • The Met — 2–3 hours on the highlights; closed on some Mondays
  • Lunch — the Upper East Side or a museum café — then take the 6 downtown
  • SoHo, the Village or the High Line — pick one: shopping and cast iron, cosy streets and coffee, or elevated park and Chelsea Market
  • Final dinner — somewhere you have wanted since day one — book it on night two

◆

Book these three things first

Statue City Cruises ferry, the observation deck slot and the Broadway show. Everything else in this plan can move around those three commitments.

☂

Wet-weather swap

If day 3 is raining, replace the park with the American Museum of Natural History and keep the downtown afternoon. Both are indoors and a short walk apart.

$

Rough sightseeing budget

Observation deck $42–46, Statue ferry $25.50, 9/11 Museum $24–36, Broadway $80–160, subway $8–10 a day. A 3-day pass rarely beats buying these four individually.

Before you go

Three-day trip questions

Is 3 days enough for the Statue of Liberty and a Broadway show?

Yes — both fit comfortably on this plan. Book the first ferry of the morning and a show on night one or two, then leave the third evening free.

What should I skip on a short trip?

Museum marathons, the Bronx and Queens, and any attraction that requires a two-hour queue for a view you get free from the Staten Island Ferry (see our attractions guide).

Do I need a sightseeing pass for three days?

Usually not. On this plan you pay for four to six attractions; CityPASS only makes sense if you intend to visit five. See the pass comparison for the maths.

Can I do this itinerary without the subway?

Not comfortably. Day 2 is walkable, days 1 and 3 rely on short rides. Load a contactless card or phone wallet for the subway — see getting around.
