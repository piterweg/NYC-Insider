Boston from New York

Three and a half to four hours each way, which makes Boston the trip on this list that most deserves a night. Come for the Freedom Trail and the North End, stay for a morning in Beacon Hill, and take the train home unhurried.

The honest answer

The city that punishes a day trip

Boston is a compact, walkable city with a genuinely great historic core. It is also about four hours away, which means a same-day visit spends about eight hours on a train for four or five hours on the ground. Stay the night and the arithmetic works.

With an afternoon and a morning you can walk the Freedom Trail from Boston Common through Beacon Hill to the North End, eat properly, and still have time for the Public Garden or a short trip to Cambridge. That is the version of Boston that makes sense from New York.

The train is the civilised way to do it. It leaves from Penn Station and arrives at South Station or Back Bay, both within a walk or short ride of the centre. Flying is about an hour and a quarter in the air, but with roughly two hours of airport time at each end it rarely saves anything.

◆

Book the return train when you book the outward one

Friday and Sunday services fill first. Buy both legs at the same time while the fares are low.

◷

One night, two half-days

Arrive early afternoon and leave late morning two days later and you get two full half-days.

Getting there

Getting there: train or plane

Amtrak runs direct from Penn Station to South Station and Back Bay. Flying is quicker in the air and slower door to door.

What to do

What the day is really about

Boston is small enough to walk and old enough to reward looking up. These four cover the historic core without turning the visit into a march.

The trail

The Freedom Trail

A painted line through the city linking the historic sites, from Boston Common through the old State House and on to the North End. Walk it in one direction rather than doubling back.

Neighbourhood

Beacon Hill

Brick sidewalks, gas lamps and narrow streets above the Common. Acorn Street is the photograph everyone takes; the side streets around Louisburg Square are the reason to linger.

Green

Boston Common and the Public Garden

The two parks at the centre of the city, one formal and one plain. The Public Garden's pond and bridge are the calmest half-hour in downtown Boston.

Food

The North End

Boston's Italian neighbourhood, with pastry shops, trattorias and the Paul Revere House. It is the best place in the city to eat, and busiest between noon and two.

Sample day

A realistic Boston overnight

One night, two half-days: the version of Boston that fits the journey. Times are indicative — check current train schedules before you book.

  • 08:00 — Train from Penn Station

    An early Amtrak from Moynihan Train Hall; the fastest services take around three and a half hours to South Station or Back Bay.

  • 12:30 — Arrive and eat

    Drop the bag at the hotel and have lunch near the Common or in the North End. Do not start with a museum — the walking is the point.

  • 13:30 — The Freedom Trail

    Walk from Boston Common through Beacon Hill to the old State House and down towards the waterfront, following the painted line.

  • 16:00 — The North End

    Paul Revere House, the old streets and a pastry. This is the neighbourhood to slow down in rather than tick off.

  • 19:30 — Dinner in the North End

    Book somewhere small and Italian, or take a chance on a trattoria with a queue. It is the most Boston evening available.

  • Day 2, 09:30 — Public Garden, then the train home

    A morning walk through the Public Garden, Beacon Hill or Cambridge, an early lunch, and an afternoon train back to New York.

◆

Do not attempt this as a day trip

Eight hours of travel for four hours in the city is a poor exchange. One night doubles the usable time.

◷

Walk the trail early

The Freedom Trail is busiest from late morning. Start at the Common around nine and you will have Beacon Hill's streets almost to yourself before the groups arrive.

☂

Stay near the centre

A hotel within walking distance of the Common, Back Bay or South Station saves a day of subway fares and keeps the trip feeling small, which is Boston's advantage over New York.

Questions

Day-trip questions

Is Boston a good day trip from New York?

Not really. The fastest trains take about three and a half hours, so a same-day return means roughly eight hours of travel for four or five hours in the city. Stay one night at least.

Should I take the train or fly to Boston?

The train is usually easier door to door. Flying takes about an hour and a quarter in the air, but the airport time at each end adds up to roughly two hours. The train also puts you in the middle of the city.

How long do you need in Boston?

One night is the minimum: an afternoon on the Freedom Trail and an evening in the North End, then a morning in Beacon Hill or Cambridge. Two nights lets you add a museum or a waterfront walk.

When is the best time to visit Boston?

Friday to Sunday for a weekend away, or a weekday for calmer streets and cheaper trains. Check current fares and schedules before booking.
