Is the New York Pass worth it?

First, the name: “New York Pass” is the old brand for what Go City now sells as its All-Inclusive pass. The current choice is between CityPASS, Go City Explorer, Essentials and All-Inclusive.

Break-even

The only question that matters

◆

CityPASS wins for five

Five expensive sights — Empire State, Top of the Rock, Edge, the 9/11 Museum and the Statue ferry — cost about $181 at the gate; CityPASS is about $151.

◆

All-Inclusive needs a busy day

At $169 for one day, the all-inclusive pass only makes sense if you would genuinely pay for several sights that day. Most visitors do not.

Run your own list

Practical

How to decide in five minutes

  • 01

    Write down the paid sights

    Only the ones you would visit without a pass. Free sights do not count towards the break-even.

  • 02

    Add their gate prices

    Use our checked price data for New York attractions, then compare with each pass price.

  • 03

    Check the number of days

    An all-inclusive pass rewards two or more paid sights a day. If you plan one big sight per day, a fixed bundle is usually better.

  • 04

    Do not buy before the list

    Buying a pass first and “using it up” is how people end up paying for attractions they never wanted.

Questions

New York Pass questions

Does the New York Pass still exist?

Not as a separate product. Go City now sells the All-Inclusive pass, which is the successor to the old New York Pass brand.

Is it worth it for three days?

Only if you would visit at least two paid attractions per day. For one or two big sights a day, CityPASS or Explorer usually wins.

Is CityPASS better?

For five expensive attractions, usually yes. For three or four sights, Explorer can be cheaper. Run the numbers on your list.
