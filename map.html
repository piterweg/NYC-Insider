    How to use it

    Plan by cluster, not by list

    New York looks small on a map and takes longer than you think. The winning move is one neighbourhood per half-day.

    Use the filters to hide categories you do not care about. Pick two or three pins that sit close together, then check the nearest subway station in the guide for that place — all of our attraction pages list it under the quick facts at the top.

    Three clusters that work especially well: Lower Manhattan (9/11 Memorial, One World Observatory, the Statue ferry, the Brooklyn Bridge), Midtown (the Empire State, Top of the Rock, MoMA, Times Square) and Brooklyn north (DUMBO, Brooklyn Bridge Park, Williamsburg).

    Food pins are the cheap, well-known spots from our food guide — useful as anchor points when you are deciding which neighbourhood to walk next.

    See the day-by-day itineraries
    ◆

    The map does not do routing

    It is a planning aid, not a navigation app. For live directions use Citymapper or Google Maps; both handle subway disruptions better than any static map.

    ◷

    Walking times are longer than they look

    Allow 15–20 minutes per avenue block cluster in Midtown, and remember that the subway is often faster than walking between 14th and 59th Streets during the day.

    ☂

    Rain changes the plan

    If it pours, head for a cluster with an indoor anchor — the Met and Central Park, MoMA and Midtown, or the 9/11 Museum and Lower Manhattan.
