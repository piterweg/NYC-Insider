Theatre District

Broadway tickets, without the guesswork

Broadway tickets are not one price but a range, and the range depends on how you buy. Here is how the system works, what TKTS and rush really save, and when full price makes sense.

How it works

One show, four ways to buy a seat

Every Broadway theatre sells the same seat at several prices over a run's life. Knowing which window you are buying in matters more than the show you pick.

Broadway runs on a weekly schedule: evenings usually at 19:00 or 20:00, matinees on Wednesday and Saturday afternoons and on Sunday, and most shows dark on Monday, some on Tuesday. Theatres cluster between 41st and 54th Streets around Times Square.

Tickets come from the show's official site, Telecharge or Broadway Direct, the box office, or discount channels such as TKTS, rush and lotteries. The seat is the same; the price and the certainty are not.

The options

Where the savings actually are

Four routes to a seat, each trading certainty for money.

Buy ahead

Official advance

The full-price route, booked days or weeks ahead. The average paid Broadway ticket in 2025–26 was $131.09 — roughly what a decent seat costs when you choose the date.

Same day

TKTS

Same-day seats up to 50% off, plus some next-day matinees. Two booths: Times Square and Lincoln Center. Fees are added, and the best-selling shows may have nothing left.

Last minute

Rush tickets

Cheap seats released on the day: digital rush through TodayTix from around 09:00 (~$30–50), or the box office at opening (~$40 cash). Availability depends on the show.

The gamble

Lotteries and standing room

Lotteries via TodayTix, Broadway Direct or Lucky Seat cost ~$30–45 and are drawn the day before or morning of. When a show sells out, standing room may be sold on the day (~$30–45, partial view).

How to work the queue

Check the board before you join the line, have a second choice ready, and go on a weeknight if you can — the selection is better and the queue shorter.

Timing

When to book what

The right moment depends on whether you are buying certainty or a discount.

  • 01

    Three months or more

    Holidays, prime weekends and a specific popular show. Book when tickets go on sale.

  • 02

    Two to three weeks

    The normal window for a named show on a chosen night. The best seats go first.

  • 03

    The day before

    Digital lotteries open in the days before a performance. Enter them all — the tickets are cheap.

  • 04

    The day of

    TKTS, rush and standing room: the cheapest route, with the least certainty.

★

What to see, by category

Long-running musicals are the easiest place to find a midweek discount. Plays and limited runs should be booked ahead. Family shows sell weekend matinees first, so book early.

Seats and venues

What you are actually paying for

Seat quality is mostly about how much of the stage you can see. Orchestra centre is the classic view; the front mezzanine is often better value for a musical, because you see the full stage at once. The balcony is cheaper and further away, and the extreme sides of any level are usually partial view.

Theatres vary in size, so check the specific house; in most, premium seats are the front orchestra and centre mezzanine, while the rear mezzanine and side orchestra are where discounts appear. A partial-view seat is fine for a play, not a dance-heavy musical. Arrive 15–20 minutes early for security; phones off, no photos or outside food.

Broadway Week

The 2-for-1 window

★

NYC Broadway Week: 2-for-1, twice a year

NYC Tourism runs Broadway Week in late summer and winter: 2-for-1 tickets, meaning 50% off each when you buy at least two, including fees. The fall 2026 edition has ended; the winter 2027 edition is next, with dates and shows published shortly before it begins. We do not print unconfirmed dates — check the official NYC Broadway Week page.

Outside those windows, TKTS is the closest thing to a citywide discount.

Questions

Broadway ticket questions

How much do Broadway tickets cost?

The average paid Broadway ticket in the 2025–26 season was $131.09. That is the middle of the market: TKTS seats are up to 50% off, rush and lottery tickets run roughly $30–50, and premium seats cost more.

What is TKTS and where are the booths?

TKTS sells same-day seats, plus some next-day matinees, at up to 50% off, with fees added. The Times Square booth (Father Duffy Square) is open Monday, Tuesday and Friday 3–8pm and Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday 11am–8pm; Lincoln Center runs Tuesday to Saturday 11am–6pm.

How do rush and lottery tickets work?

Rush tickets are cheap seats released on the day: digital rush through TodayTix from around 09:00, or at the box office when it opens, typically around $30–50. Digital lotteries through TodayTix, Broadway Direct or Lucky Seat cost roughly $30–45 and are drawn the day before or morning of.

When should I book Broadway tickets?

For a specific show on a specific night, book two to three weeks ahead — longer for holidays and prime weekends. If the show matters less than the night out, TKTS and rush are cheaper.

What is NYC Broadway Week?

It is NYC Tourism's 2-for-1 programme: 50% off each ticket when you buy at least two, including fees. It runs twice a year, in late summer and winter. The fall 2026 edition has ended; winter 2027 dates and shows are published shortly before it begins — check the official page.
