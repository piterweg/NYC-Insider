The best pizza in New York

New York did not invent pizza, but it turned it into a daily habit: a slice on a paper plate, eaten standing up, for less than the price of a coffee. Here is how the styles differ and how to order without looking like a visitor.

9 placesSlice $4–5.50All five boroughs
Why it is its own thing

A city built on the slice

Pizza in New York is less a cuisine than a format: big, thin, foldable and sold by the slice at almost any hour.

The city's pizzerias fall into a few families — the neighbourhood slice counter, the Sicilian square, the coal-fired institution and the wood-fired Neapolitan newcomer — and each has its own rules. What they share is scale: a whole 18-inch pie usually costs $28–38, a plain slice $4–5.50, which is why pizza became the city's default lunch.

Geography matters too. The famous places are scattered across Manhattan, Brooklyn and Queens, and eating very well without leaving Manhattan is easy — but the pilgrimage is the point.

Know what you are ordering

The four styles

Names on menus are not consistent. What matters is the shape, the oven and the way it is served.

The default

The slice

Large, thin and wide, cut from a round pie and sold by the piece. Folded lengthways and eaten on the move; $4–5.50.

Thick and rectangular

Square / Sicilian

Baked in a rectangular pan, with a thick, airy base and crisp bottom. Often the sauce goes on top of the cheese.

Blistered and thin

Coal-fired

Very thin, charred at the edges and baked fast at high heat. Usually a whole pie, and usually worth the queue.

Soft and puffed

Neapolitan

Wood-fired, softer and wetter in the centre, with a puffed rim. Whole pies only, ideally with a knife and fork.

The slice and pie prices are typical 2026 figures; the bands are honest ranges, not quotes. Menus move, and a wood-fired pie is a different purchase from a counter slice. Confirm at the door.

How to order

Order like a local

There is no secret handshake, but there is a rhythm. These five habits will get you a better slice and a shorter wait.

  • 01

    Order at the counter, not the table

    At a slice shop you queue, order and carry your own food. Table service means a whole-pie restaurant — slower, and a different experience.

  • 02

    Ask for a fresh slice if you want it hot

    A "regular" slice comes from a pie already on the counter and gets reheated. If you want one straight from the oven, ask for a fresh slice.

  • 03

    Fold it lengthways

    The fold keeps the cheese on and lets you eat with one hand. If the slice collapses, it needed another minute.

  • 04

    Put your name down for a whole pie

    Coal-fired and wood-fired places take no reservations; expect 20–40 minutes at peak times. Give a name, take a walk, come back when they call it.

  • 05

    Tip the counter, not the table

    A dollar or two in the jar is normal at a slice counter. At a sit-down pizzeria, tip 18–22% like any other restaurant.

Questions

Pizza questions, answered

Is New York pizza really better than pizza elsewhere?

It is different rather than objectively better. The slice format — large, thin, foldable and cheap — makes everyday pizza possible in a way most cities cannot match, and the best coal-fired and wood-fired places are world-class.

How much should I pay for a slice?

A plain slice is typically $4–5.50 in 2026, and a whole 18-inch pie $28–38. A specialty square or a wood-fired pie costs more. Anything far above that pays for the address.

What is the difference between a slice and a square?

A slice is cut from a large thin round pie and folded. A square, or Sicilian, is baked in a rectangular pan with a thicker base, often with the sauce over the cheese.

Do I need to book a table?

Slice counters and most pizzerias are walk-in only. The coal-fired and wood-fired destinations do not take reservations either; you queue and wait 20–40 minutes at busy times. Going off-peak is the only real trick.

Which pizza place is closest to the tourist sights?

Joe's in Greenwich Village is a short walk from Washington Square Park, and Prince Street Pizza is in the middle of SoHo. For the Brooklyn Bridge, Juliana's and Grimaldi's are both in DUMBO.
