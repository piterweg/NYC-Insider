The Rockefeller Christmas Tree, 2026

A 76-foot Norway spruce from West Edmeston, New York; lit on Wednesday 2 December and on view through mid-January 2027. Here is how to see it without wasting an evening in a crowd.

Visiting

How to see it without the crush

◆

Go early or late

The plaza is busiest from late afternoon to evening. Early morning is calmer; late evening after the theatre crowds is also better.

◆

Skip the lighting ceremony

The 2 December ceremony draws huge crowds and road closures. See the tree the next day instead — it stays up for weeks.

  • 01

    View from the plaza, not just the street

    The tree is best from inside Rockefeller Plaza and from The Rink. Fifth Avenue gives you the crowd, not the view.

  • 02

    Skating is separate

    The Rink requires a timed ticket and is not included with tree viewing. Book ahead for December dates.

  • 03

    Top of the Rock is optional

    A Holiday Top of the Rock ticket combines the observation deck with tree access, from about $49. The tree itself is free.

  • 04

    Expect security and crowds

    Bag checks and barriers are normal in December. Leave extra time and avoid the immediate post-ceremony evening.

Questions

Rockefeller tree questions

When is the 2026 Rockefeller tree lit?

Wednesday 2 December 2026, during the evening lighting ceremony. The tree remains on view through mid-January 2027.

Is it free to see the tree?

Yes. Viewing from the plaza and The Rink is free; skating and Top of the Rock tickets are separate.

Where is the tree from?

The 2026 tree is a 76-foot Norway spruce from West Edmeston, New York.

When is the best time to visit?

Early morning or late evening on a weekday, after the lighting ceremony and before the Christmas week peak.
