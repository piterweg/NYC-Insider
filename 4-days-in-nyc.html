Four days in New York

Three Manhattan days at a calmer pace, then a full day across the river. Four days is where a trip stops being a checklist and starts to feel like a visit.

The shape of the trip

Three days of icons, one day in Brooklyn

The first three days take the classic Manhattan plan at a gentler speed: skyline, harbour, park and one museum. The fourth crosses the river.

The extra day is best spent in Brooklyn: DUMBO, the waterfront and Williamsburg give you the skyline from the other side.

Keep one hotel for all four nights. Brooklyn is 10–20 minutes from Lower Manhattan by subway.

The plan

Four days, day by day

Days one to three are Manhattan and work from any Midtown or Lower Manhattan base. Day four is Brooklyn from the first stop to dinner.

01 Midtown

Arrive, go up, come down to earth

Keep the arrival day light: one skyline, one walk, one dinner — nothing that cannot move.

  • Empire State Building — The 86th floor from $46; it opens at 10:00 on most winter weekdays and the first slot skips the queue.
  • Bryant Park and Grand Central — Free. Walk south past the library and Bryant Park, then under Grand Central's ceiling.
  • Lunch in Midtown East — A deli or bagel counter around $10–18; faster than the restaurants on the squares.
  • Times Square at dusk — Fifteen minutes of billboards, then dinner or a show. Same-day TKTS seats run up to 50% off.
02 Harbour & downtown

Statue, memorial and the bridge

The busiest day, but tight: buy the ferry ticket ahead and be at Battery Park before 09:00.

  • Statue City Cruises ferry — From Battery Park to Liberty and Ellis Island. Official tickets from $25.50; allow four to five hours.
  • Lunch near the Seaport — Fulton Street or the Seaport, five minutes from the ferry terminal.
  • 9/11 Memorial and Museum — The plaza is free. The museum is timed from $24 and takes 45–90 minutes; closed most Tuesdays.
  • Brooklyn Bridge and dinner — Walk the bridge at golden hour, 30–40 minutes each way, then eat in the Village or Seaport.
03 Uptown

Central Park, the Met and a neighbourhood

One museum and one long park walk, then a downtown neighbourhood — you finish near good food either way.

  • Central Park — Enter at 72nd Street and walk the Mall to Bethesda Terrace and the Bow Bridge. Free.
  • The Met — Two to three hours on the highlights. $30, and closed Wednesdays — check the day before.
  • The High Line or a neighbourhood — Pick one: the High Line and Chelsea Market, or SoHo and Greenwich Village on foot.
  • Final Manhattan dinner — Book it with the show. The last night is the one people leave to chance.
04 Brooklyn

The other side of the river

Subway to High Street and stay in Brooklyn all day. No Manhattan stops — that is the point.

  • DUMBO and Washington Street — The bridge framed between the warehouses, early, before the photograph queue builds.
  • Brooklyn Bridge Park and the promenade — Lawns, piers and the best skyline view from Brooklyn, then the Heights walk. Free.
  • Lunch in Williamsburg — Bedford Avenue for pizza, bagels or something newer, then the waterfront.
  • Domino Park and dinner — The old sugar refinery site, with the skyline across the river. Eat in Williamsburg.

Make it work

What makes or breaks four days

  • 01

    Give Brooklyn its own day

    DUMBO, the waterfront and Williamsburg deserve more than an afternoon bolted onto the harbour day.

  • 02

    Book only the fixed points

    Four days tempts people into booking everything. Book the ferry, one deck and one show; leave the rest open.

  • 03

    Use the last day as a buffer

    If the weather turns, swap the Brooklyn day with day three. Both flex except for the Met's Wednesday closure.

  • 04

    Keep one hotel

    Changing hotels costs half a day in packing and trains. A Manhattan base holds for all four nights.

Questions

Four-day trip questions

Is four days enough for New York?

Four days covers the icons, one big museum, the Statue of Liberty and a Brooklyn day. Not the Bronx, Queens or a day trip.

What should I cut if I only have three days?

Cut the Brooklyn day. Days one to three are the classic Manhattan plan and work alone; the 3-day itinerary runs in the same order.

Should I move hotels for the Brooklyn day?

No. Brooklyn is 10–20 minutes from Lower Manhattan on the subway, and changing hotels costs more time than the trip saves.

Is a sightseeing pass worth it for four days?

Only if your shortlist reaches five paid attractions. CityPASS is $149 for five from ten; this plan pays for four or five. See the pass comparison.

What if it rains during the trip?

Swap the Brooklyn day with day three and put the Met, MoMA or AMNH on the wet day. The bridge is still walkable in rain.
