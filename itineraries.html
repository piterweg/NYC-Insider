How we plan

Four rules behind every plan

  • 01

    One area per half-day

    New York looks small on a map and is enormous in practice. Clustering stops by neighbourhood saves you hours on the subway.

  • 02

    Book the big things first

    Observation decks, the Statue of Liberty and Broadway are the three fixed points. Everything else can move around them.

  • 03

    Protect one meal a day

    Plan breakfast or lunch properly and leave dinner loose. The best New York meals are rarely the ones you booked a month ahead.

  • 04

    Walk more than you ride in Manhattan

    Above 59th Street, walking the grid is usually faster than going underground. Below it, the subway is your friend.

◷

Leave the first hour empty

Jet lag, a hotel queue, a wrong turn out of Penn Station — something always eats the first morning. Our plans start with a soft stop so a late start does not break the day.

☂

Have a rain plan

New York rain is heavy and short. Keep one indoor anchor per day — a museum, a market, a food hall — so a wet afternoon does not ruin anything.

◆

Check opening days

The Met closes on some Mondays, MoMA is open daily, the Statue of Liberty ferries sell out, and Broadway plays dark on different nights. Our pages note the usual schedules — always confirm before booking.

Questions

Itinerary questions we get a lot

Is three days enough for New York?

Three full days is enough to see the headline sights without feeling rushed, as long as you pick one area per half-day. You will not do the museums and the outer boroughs, and you should not try.

Should I book Broadway tickets in advance?

For a specific show or a weekend, yes — two to three weeks ahead is sensible. For midweek flexibility, TKTS same-day discounts or the show's own rush lottery are often cheaper.

Where should I stay for a first trip?

Midtown is convenient and anonymous; the Village, SoHo and the Upper West Side have more character and better food. Anywhere within a five-minute walk of a subway line works. Avoid hotels that are only near a far-flung station.

Do these itineraries work with kids?

Yes, with cuts. The 5-day plan minus one museum day and with an earlier dinner works well. The Staten Island Ferry, Central Park playgrounds and the American Museum of Natural History are the reliable wins.

What if it rains?

Swap the outdoor block for a museum, a market or a food hall. The plan pages suggest a wet-weather alternative for each day.
