SUMMIT One Vanderbilt

Part observation deck, part art installation. You come for the skyline and spend most of the visit looking at the city reflected in the floor, the ceiling and everything in between.

The visit

The deck that turns the city into a mirror

SUMMIT sits on top of One Vanderbilt, directly above Grand Central Terminal. The route runs through three levels — Transcendence, Affinity and Levitation — and each one uses glass and mirror to do something to the view before it lets you see it.

The installations are the reason this deck feels different from the others. The mirrors do not just reflect you; they reflect the skyline back into the building, so the boundary between inside and outside stops being obvious. It is the most photogenic interior in Midtown, and it is busy because of it.

The actual view is exceptional too. Central Park runs north, the Chrysler Building and the Empire State Building stand close by, and Grand Central's roof is directly below. The mirrors amplify all of it, but the city is doing the work.

Why go

Why SUMMIT is worth the ticket

If the other decks are about height, this one is about the experience of looking.

01

Rooms built for photographs

Transcendence and Affinity are installations as much as viewing floors. The mirrored surfaces double the skyline and the crowd, and the effect in late afternoon light is the reason the sunset slots cost more.

02

A view directly over Grand Central

The terminal's roof is immediately below, and beyond it the Chrysler Building, the Empire State and the full run of Central Park. No other midtown deck puts you this close to the buildings you are looking at.

03

Levitation and the glass below you

The highest floor is where you look down between your feet and find the street. It is a controlled version of the Edge glass floor, and it changes how the rest of the visit feels.

Tickets and entry

Tickets, entry and what to wear

Timed entry only, bag check on arrival, and one clothing rule worth knowing before you leave the hotel.

  1. Book timed entry on the official SUMMIT site. The price is around $44–62 and rises for weekend and sunset slots.
  2. Decide about SUMMIT Ascent, the glass elevator, at the same time. It is an add-on rather than part of general admission.
  3. Wear trousers, jeans or shorts. The mirrored floors reflect everything, and complimentary shoe covers are handed out at the entrance.
  4. Leave large bags and strollers behind. Security and the bag check will slow you down, and the route is not built for them.
  5. Arrive via Grand Central–42 St and walk to the Vanderbilt Avenue entrance at 42nd Street. The first slot of the day is the calmest.
◆

Good to know

The deck opens around 09:00, with the last entry around 20:00 and sometimes later on Friday and Saturday nights. Those times vary by season and demand, so check the official site for current hours before you book a fixed dinner reservation around it.

Insider tips

What most visitors get wrong

  • 01

    Wear the right clothes

    Skirts and loose dresses are a genuine problem on the mirrored floors. Trousers, jeans or shorts make the whole visit easier, and shoe covers are provided for everyone.

  • 02

    Book the first slot of the day

    The mirrors work best when there is less crowd to reflect, and the early slot is the quietest and cheapest. By late afternoon the top floor is slow to move through.

  • 03

    Decide on Ascent before you arrive

    The glass elevator is a paid extra, not something you can add cheaply at the last minute. If it matters to you, buy it with the ticket.

  • 04

    Treat it as an experience, not just a view

    People who rush to the windows miss the point. The installations are what separate SUMMIT from the four other decks, and they reward a slower walk through.

Questions

SUMMIT questions

What should I wear to SUMMIT?

Trousers, jeans or shorts. The floors and ceilings are mirrored, so skirts and dresses leave you exposed in the reflection. Complimentary shoe covers are provided, and you keep your shoes on underneath.

How long does a visit take?

Allow 1.5–2 hours for the three floors. There is more to look at than the view, so people tend to move through it more slowly than a conventional observation deck.

Is SUMMIT Ascent included in the ticket?

No. Ascent, the glass elevator ride, is a paid add-on. Buy it with your entry ticket rather than deciding at the top, when the remaining slots may not suit you.

Is SUMMIT good for children?

Older children tend to enjoy the mirrors and the glass; very young children get less from it and the bag rules make strollers awkward. Large bags and strollers are not allowed, and there is a bag check on site.

When is the quietest slot?

The first slot of the morning and the last slots of the evening. Sunset is the most expensive and the busiest, because the mirrors pick up the light and the queues build on the top floor.
