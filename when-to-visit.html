When to visit

The best time to go to New York

New York has four proper seasons, and each changes what the trip feels like. The best month depends on whether you want long park days, Christmas windows or quiet museums.

Seasons

Four seasons, four different cities

Central Park averages about 50 inches of rain a year across roughly 125 wet days, so rain is possible in any month. What changes is the temperature.

Spring

March–May

Cool and changeable, warming from the low 50s to the low 70s. The park fills with blossom in late April; May is one of the most pleasant months.

Layers and an umbrella
Summer

June–August

Hot and humid, with highs in the low 80s and heavy afternoon storms. Long evenings, free outdoor concerts and busy park weekends.

Light clothes and water
Autumn

September–November

The best window of the year. Warm, clear days through September and early October, then crisp air and foliage as November arrives.

A light jacket and good shoes
Winter

December–February

Cold, with highs in the 30s and 40s and occasional snow. December is atmosphere and crowds; January and February are quiet, cheap and coldest.

A proper coat and boots

Best months

When to book, and when to avoid

Two windows stand out: mid-April to mid-June, and September to early October. Both give you warm days, comfortable evenings and the city at its most usable.

Late spring means blossom in the park, outdoor tables and long daylight. Early autumn means warm water still in the harbour, fewer crowds than summer and the best walking weather of the year. If you want Christmas atmosphere, December delivers it, at a price in crowds and hotel rates.

July and August are hot and humid, with afternoon storms and crowded parks. January and February are the cheapest and coldest weeks, when museums are peaceful and Restaurant Week offers good-value menus.

◆

Best for weather

Mid-April to mid-June and September to early October. Comfortable temperatures, manageable rain and the park at its best.

★

Best for atmosphere

Early December. The tree at Rockefeller Center, shop windows and the Bryant Park Winter Village, with cold but rarely brutal weather.

◷

Best for value

Late January and early February. The lowest room rates of the year, quiet museums and Restaurant Week prix-fixe menus.

Holidays

Holiday travel, honestly

The holiday weeks are the most atmospheric and the most expensive. Whether they are worth it depends on how much you value atmosphere against crowds.

Pros and cons of travelling to New York in holiday periods
PeriodUpsideDownside
Thanksgiving weekParade, early Christmas windows, crisp weatherBusiest travel days of the year; hotel rates peak
Christmas to New YearTree, lights, ice rinks and a genuine sense of occasionHighest room rates, long queues, reduced hours on the holiday days themselves
SummerLong evenings, free concerts, parks and beachesHeat and humidity, afternoon storms, peak hotel pricing
January–FebruaryCheapest rooms, quiet museums, Restaurant WeekThe coldest weeks; short daylight hours
◆

Book holiday trips early

For Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year, start looking four to six months ahead. The best-value rooms in those weeks disappear first, and flights only get more expensive.

☂

Expect reduced hours

Some restaurants and attractions close or run shorter hours on Thanksgiving Day and Christmas Day. If either is a centrepiece of your trip, confirm opening hours and book ahead rather than assuming.

◷

New Year's Eve is a commitment

The Times Square ball drop is free but means arriving early afternoon, standing for hours and passing security screening. Many New Yorkers watch it on television instead.

Packing

What to pack by season

New York is a walking city, so the most important packing decision is footwear. Everything else follows the season.

What to pack for each New York season
SeasonPack
Winter (Dec–Feb)Insulated coat, hat, gloves, scarf, warm layers, waterproof boots with grip, moisturiser for dry air
Spring (Mar–May)Layers, a light waterproof jacket, an umbrella, comfortable walking shoes, sunglasses
Summer (Jun–Aug)Light breathable clothing, sun hat, sunscreen, refillable water bottle, sandals for the park
Autumn (Sep–Nov)Light jacket or coat, sweaters, an umbrella, closed walking shoes for wet leaves
◆

Shoes beat everything

You will walk 10,000–20,000 steps on a full sightseeing day. Bring broken-in shoes and a second pair in case one gets wet.

☂

Rain is short, not rare

Around 125 wet days a year means rain is always possible. A compact umbrella and a waterproof layer are more useful than a heavy raincoat, because most storms pass in an hour or two.

Questions

When-to-visit questions

What is the best month to visit New York?

May and September are the safest answers: warm, comfortable and less extreme than midsummer. Mid-April to mid-June and September to early October are the best windows.

Is New York worth visiting in winter?

Yes, if you pack for it and accept short days. December is beautiful and busy; January and February are cheap, quiet and cold.

How hot does New York get in summer?

Average highs in July and August are around 84°F (29°C), with humidity that makes it feel hotter. Afternoon storms are common and subway platforms get uncomfortable. Plan outdoor sights for the morning.

When does the Christmas season start?

Bryant Park Winter Village opens on 28 October 2026, and the Rockefeller Center tree is lit on 2 December 2026, staying up until about 9 January 2027. Early December is atmospheric and slightly less crowded than the final week of the year.

What should I avoid booking around?

The New York City Marathon closes streets in all five boroughs on the first Sunday of November, and the Thanksgiving parade shuts down part of the Upper West Side and Midtown on 26 November 2026. Check the calendar before planning an outdoor day.

Does it snow in New York?

Sometimes, but heavy snow is not guaranteed. January and February are likeliest, with average highs in the high 30s and low 40s. Waterproof boots matter more than a snow forecast.
