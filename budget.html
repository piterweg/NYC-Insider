Budget

What New York really costs

New York can be done on a careful budget or very little care. The difference is mostly accommodation, sit-down dinners and tickets — not the subway.

What the bands actually buy

A budget day is a bagel or a slice for lunch, one paid attraction, coffee and the subway — perfectly good if your hotel is cheap and your evenings are simple.

A mid-range day adds a sit-down dinner, a second attraction and a show fund. The upscale band assumes fine dining, private tours and taxis. Most visitors land between budget and mid-range, moving up for a day or two.

◆

Where the money goes

Accommodation and tickets swing most. A two-person dinner costs around $120–180 before tax; a pizza slice is $4–5.50. Transport rarely exceeds $36 a week per person.

Accommodation

What a room costs per night

Quoted rates are before tax. New York adds roughly 14.75% hotel tax plus $3.50 per night.

Read the all-in price

A room advertised at $400 a night costs around $462 with tax. On a five-night stay that is roughly $310 more — enough to change the shape of a trip. Check the total before comparing hotels.

☂

Value does not always mean Manhattan

Brooklyn, Queens and Upper Manhattan often give more space for the same money. What you lose is time on the subway, so weigh the commute against the room.

Food

Eating well without the bill

Food is where New York rewards curiosity. A slice, a bagel, a taco or a bowl of noodles costs $4–15 and is often better than a restaurant charging four times as much.

Sit-down dinners are the exception: a mid-range dinner for two runs around $120–180 before tax, or $155–230 with tax and a 20% tip. Budget one proper dinner a day and eat casually the rest of the time.

Reliable cheap eats

Joe's Pizza in Greenwich Village (slice from around $4.50), Prince Street Pizza in SoHo (pepperoni square from around $5.50), Ess-a-Bagel, Los Tacos No. 1, Xi'an Famous Foods, The Halal Guys, Vanessa's Dumpling House, Gray's Papaya, Mamoun's Falafel and Peter Pan Donuts in Greenpoint.

☂

Skip the tourist-menu trap

Restaurants in Times Square and around major sights charge a premium for convenience. Walk ten minutes and the price drops while the food improves.

Taxes and tipping

The costs on top of the bill

New York prices are usually shown before tax, and tipping is expected in sit-down restaurants and taxis.

Taxi tip15–20%

Save money

Four tactics that actually work

01

Buy the pass you will use

Count the paid attractions first. Five or more in a short trip is where CityPASS or an Explorer Pass starts to win; two or three is cheaper separately.

02

Use the free museum windows

The Whitney is free on Friday nights for everyone, free on Second Sundays, and free daily for ages 25 and under. The Brooklyn Museum has free First Saturday evenings; the Bronx Museum is always free.

03

Cap your transit

Tap the same card all week and 12 fares in seven days is the maximum you pay — around $36. Rideshare and taxis are the only way to blow the transport budget.

04

Travel in the shoulder weeks

Late January and early February are the cheapest weeks for rooms. November and early December are better value than the holiday weeks that follow.

Questions

Budget questions

Is $100 a day enough in New York?

Not comfortably. Our budget band starts at around $130–200 per person per day excluding the hotel, which covers casual food, one paid attraction and the subway. Below that you limit meals and sights.

How much should I tip in New York?

20% is the standard in sit-down restaurants, calculated on the pre-tax total. Taxis get 15–20%, bar drinks $1–2 each, housekeeping $3–5 per night and $2–5 per bag for help with luggage. Check for a service charge before adding a tip.

Is a sightseeing pass worth it?

Only if you will use it. Add up the full-price tickets for the attractions you intend to visit, then compare with the pass price. At five or more paid sights a pass usually wins; at two or three it rarely does.

What is the cheapest time to visit New York?

Late January and early February, when rooms are at their lowest and museums are quiet. The trade-off is the coldest weather of the year. November and early December offer better balance between price and comfort.
