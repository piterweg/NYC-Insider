Passes and tickets

Passes, compared honestly

A sightseeing pass is a bet that you will visit enough paid attractions to beat the ticket price. Sometimes that bet is easy money. Here are five passes, the break-even maths and a calculator.

The honest answer

Buy a pass for the trip you will take

Passes are sold on the number of attractions you could visit. They are worth it only for the number you actually will.

There are two families. Fixed bundles, such as CityPASS, give you a set number of entries for one price. Day-based passes, like Go City All-Inclusive and New York Pass, charge by the day and let you enter as much as you can. Fixed bundles suit a first visit with a clear shortlist; day-based passes suit a return visitor with the stamina for three or four paid sights a day.

Break-even maths

When a pass pays for itself

Rough sums, not guarantees, using an average of about $45 per paid attraction per person — between a $28–30 museum and a $46–51 deck.

CityPASS pays off at five attractions if those five average more than about $30 each. At $149 for five entries, that is roughly $29.80 per attraction. Five sights at the $45 average would cost about $225 at the gate, so the pass saves around $76. But a museum-only five — AMNH at $28, the Met at $30, MoMA at $30, the 9/11 Museum at $24 and a free sight — comes to far less than $149. It only wins when the five include an observation deck or the harbour ferry, and it must be used within nine days.

Go City Explorer roughly breaks even at two choices and improves as you add. At five choices ($179) it is around $46 ahead of the gate price; at ten ($299) around $151 ahead. The saving shrinks with cheaper attractions, so treat the tier price as a ceiling on value, not a promise.

Day-based passes need a punishing pace. Go City All-Inclusive starts at $169 for one day; to beat that with $45 attractions you need four entries in a single day. These passes win over three or more days of heavy use. Essentials sits in the middle: $99 for a deck and two attractions is about $135 at our average, so roughly $36 saved.

Match the pass to the trip

Which pass for which trip length

Pick by the shape of your days, not by the size of the discount.

1–2 days

Go City Essentials or nothing

A short trip rarely fits more than three paid sights. Essentials bundles a deck and two attractions for $99.

3–4 days

CityPASS or Explorer

The sweet spot. If your shortlist is the expensive icons, CityPASS wins. If the list is still moving, Explorer at four to six choices leaves room to change your mind.

5–7 days

Explorer at six to eight choices

Long enough to use eight entries without sprinting. The Explorer tier usually pulls ahead past five sights; CityPASS still works if the first five are right.

7+ days

Probably no pass

A week or more includes park mornings and repeat visits. Buy the two or three tickets you actually want; a pass used twice is worse than full-price tickets used well.

Before you buy

The prices move — treat these as starting points

Every operator here uses dynamic pricing. The “from” price applies to the cheapest dates, usually midweek in low season; weekend or holiday dates can run 20–40% higher. Price your exact dates on the operator's own site, then compare the pass against the tickets you would buy anyway — our attractions guide lists current admission prices.

◆

Check the attraction list

A pass including 101 attractions is not 101 times as useful. Check that the three or four sights you want are included before you pay — lists change.

◷

Reserve after you buy

Buying the pass does not book the time. Reserve popular observation-deck slots in the same sitting.

Questions

Pass questions we get a lot

Is CityPASS worth it for a first trip?

Usually, if your shortlist includes two or more observation decks or the Statue of Liberty ferry. It is less convincing for a museum-only trip.

Does CityPASS include the Statue of Liberty?

Yes — the Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island ferry is one of the ten CityPASS options, using one of your five choices. Book the ferry time as soon as you have the pass.

Do I still need reservations with a pass?

Yes, at most sites. A pass buys the entry, not the time. Reserve both together, and see our attractions guide for what to book first.

Are passes cheaper for children?

CityPASS has a child rate: $129 for ages 6–17 against $149 adult, plus a $2 fee. The Go City passes and New York Pass price by age band, so check their current child policy.
