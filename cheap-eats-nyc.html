Cheap eats in New York

You do not need to spend much to eat well here. The city's cheapest food is also its most characteristic: a slice, a bagel, a plate of dumplings, a taco eaten standing up.

Under $1010 reliable spotsAll five boroughs
Start here

What $10 still buys

New York is expensive, but cheap food never disappeared. It moved to the counter, the cart and the bakery — and it is often better than the restaurant two doors down.

The trick is to eat what the city eats every day rather than what it serves visitors. A plain slice is $4–5.50, a bagel with cream cheese $4.50–7, eight dumplings about $7. Add coffee at $3.50–5 and you can have a good day's eating for the price of one mid-range main course.

Prices below are typical 2026 figures and exclude the 8.875% sales tax. They are a guide, not a quote — menus move, and portions vary by neighbourhood.

Tax and tip change the total

Sales tax is 8.875% and is added at the register. At a counter, tipping is optional and a dollar or two in the jar is plenty; at a sit-down restaurant, expect 18–22%.

How to do it

Eat cheaply like a local

Five habits that keep a food day under budget without making it feel like a compromise.

  • 01

    Make lunch the main meal

    Counters and neighbourhood restaurants charge less at midday, and many run a lunch special. Eat properly at 1pm and keep dinner light.

  • 02

    Eat where people work and live

    Midtown office blocks at lunchtime, outer-borough high streets at any time. Prices fall as soon as you leave the tourist axis.

  • 03

    Order by the piece, not the combo

    A slice, two tacos, eight dumplings, a falafel sandwich. The piecemeal order is usually the cheapest and the freshest.

  • 04

    Drink tap water

    New York's tap water is free and good. A $5 soft drink or bottled water is the easiest way to double the cost of lunch.

  • 05

    Carry some cash

    Most places take cards, but a few old-school counters and market stalls still prefer cash. Twenty dollars is enough for a day of eating.

Questions

Cheap eats, answered

Can you eat cheaply in New York City?

Yes. A bagel breakfast, a slice or taco lunch and a casual dinner costs roughly $45–70 a day. That is food only; our budget guide puts a whole budget day at $130–200 including transport and one paid attraction.

What is the cheapest meal in New York?

A plain slice ($4–5.50), a bagel with cream cheese ($4.50–7) or eight dumplings (about $7). Coffee is $3.50–5, so breakfast can cost less than $10.

Do cheap places take cards?

Most do, including the counter spots on this page. A few old-school places are cash-first, so carry $20–40 rather than relying entirely on your phone.

Is street food safe to eat?

New York's food carts are regulated and busy, and turnover keeps the food fresh. Choose the cart with a queue at lunchtime. Avoid anything that has clearly been sitting out.

Where can I eat cheaply near Times Square?

Los Tacos No. 1 has a Times Square branch with tacos at $5–6 each, and The Halal Guys cart at 53rd and Sixth is a few blocks away. Both are far cheaper than the restaurants on the square itself.
