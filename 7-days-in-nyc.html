Itinerary · 7 days

Seven days in New York

A week is what it takes to stop seeing New York as a set of landmarks. This plan does the icons properly, then spends three days in the boroughs most visitors never reach — with a spare day for whatever the city makes you curious about.

7 daysAll five boroughsPublic transportOne free day
Why a week changes the trip

The boroughs are the point

Most one-week New York itineraries are five days of Manhattan with two day trips bolted on. This one is different: the first five days cover the city's headline sights, and the last two take you to Queens, the Bronx and Staten Island — the places that explain how New York actually works.

You will still climb an observation deck and see a show. You will also eat your way down Roosevelt Avenue, stand on the Grand Concourse, and take the best free boat ride in the United States. The pace is deliberately uneven: two big days, one slow day, then repeat.

The plan

Seven days, day by day

Days 1–5 follow the 5-day itinerary. Days 6 and 7 are the ones that make a week worthwhile — keep them flexible and check opening days before you travel.

01 Midtown

Arrive and take the skyline in

Settle in, then go up while the light is good. Evening in the Theatre District.

  • Top of the Rock or Empire State Building — book a late-afternoon slot
  • Bryant Park & Grand Central — walk through the concourse at rush hour
  • Times Square at dusk — 15 minutes, then leave
  • Broadway show — book ahead; Thursday is a good night
02 Harbour

Statue of Liberty and Lower Manhattan

The one morning that must start early — the first Statue City Cruises ferry.

  • Statue of Liberty & Ellis Island — first ferry from Battery Park
  • Lunch at the Seaport — Tin Building or Pier 17
  • 9/11 Memorial & Museum — plaza free; museum ~2 hours
  • Wall Street & Stone Street — short walking loop
  • Brooklyn Bridge at sunset — walk into DUMBO, eat there
03 Park & museum

Central Park, The Met and the Upper East Side

The Met is closed on Wednesdays — put this day on any other weekday.

  • Central Park — The Mall → Bethesda Terrace → Bow Bridge
  • The Met — three hours on the highlights
  • Fifth Avenue & Museum Mile — Guggenheim exterior, Conservatory Garden
  • Dinner on the Upper West Side — Amsterdam Avenue or Columbus
04 Brooklyn

A full Brooklyn day

One borough, one direction: north Brooklyn in the morning, Prospect Park in the afternoon.

  • Brooklyn Heights Promenade — morning skyline walk
  • DUMBO — Washington Street view, Brooklyn Bridge Park
  • Williamsburg — Domino Park, Bedford Avenue, lunch
  • Prospect Park — Long Meadow and the Botanic Garden
  • Dinner in Park Slope — Fifth Avenue
05 Downtown

SoHo, the Village and the High Line

The downtown day. Walk more than you ride, and keep the evening free for a return visit.

  • SoHo & Nolita — Greene Street, Prince Street, coffee
  • Greenwich Village — Washington Square, Bleecker Street
  • High Line — enter at Gansevoort or 23rd Street
  • Chelsea Market — lunch or early dinner
  • Madison Square Park — sunset and the Flatiron photo
06 Queens

The most diverse food day in America

No observation decks today. The 7 train is the itinerary: Long Island City, Jackson Heights, Flushing.

  • Gantry Plaza State Park — Long Island City waterfront, morning skyline
  • MoMA PS1 or the Noguchi Museum — pick one — PS1 is closed some weekdays
  • Jackson Heights — Little India on 74th Street, lunch on Roosevelt Avenue
  • Flushing — New World Mall food court, Main Street
  • Flushing Meadows–Corona Park — the Unisphere and the Queens Museum
07 Bronx & Staten Island

The other New York

A long but memorable final day. Start north, finish on the water, and keep the evening loose.

  • Yankee Stadium area — 161 St–Yankee Stadium; the stadium tour if there is no game
  • Bronx Zoo or the NY Botanical Garden — allow 2–3 hours
  • Arthur Avenue — lunch at the retail market, Mike's Deli, Zero Otto Nove
  • Staten Island Ferry — free, from Whitehall Terminal, 25 minutes each way
  • Snug Harbor — Chinese Scholar's Garden and the grounds, if time allows
  • Last dinner — back in Manhattan — book it

◆

Keep day 7 flexible

The Bronx and Staten Island day is long. If you are flagging, choose one: the zoo and Arthur Avenue, or the ferry and Snug Harbor. Both are excellent; neither is worth a forced march.

☂

Rain plan

On a wet day, swap Central Park for AMNH, the High Line for a museum, and the zoo for the Bronx Museum of the Arts (free, Wednesday–Sunday). Keep the ferry — it runs in rain and the harbour is dramatic.

◷

Book in this order

Broadway, Statue of Liberty ferry, two observation decks, The Met. Everything else can be decided the day before. The Queens and Bronx days need no reservations at all.

Questions

One-week trip questions

Is seven days too long for a first visit?

No — it is the first length that lets you slow down. If you only want the icons, the 5-day plan is enough. Seven days is for people who want to understand the city.

Do I need a car for Queens and the Bronx?

No. The 7 train, the D and 4, and the Staten Island Ferry cover everything on this plan. Driving in New York is a separate hobby.

Should I buy a seven-day sightseeing pass?

Probably not on this plan. You pay for six or seven attractions over the week, which is the break-even zone — CityPASS plus a couple of single tickets usually beats an all-inclusive pass. See the pass comparison.

What about day trips to Philadelphia or Boston?

Not on this itinerary. Both are full days on the train and deserve their own trips. A week in New York is better spent in New York.
